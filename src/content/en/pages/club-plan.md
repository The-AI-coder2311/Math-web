---
title: The Club Plan, Weeks 1 to 16
description: A week-by-week plan for a beginner math club, with one 45-minute meeting per week for 16 weeks, two mock contests, and no competition background needed.
---

This plan assumes that your students have never competed and that you haven't either. Read the week's answer key before the meeting. That's all the preparation you need.

## Every meeting follows the same 45 minutes

| Time | Activity | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| 5 min | **Warm-up** | Put the warm-up question on the board. Students try it as they come in. |
| 10 min | **Big idea** | Work through the week's idea together, using the talking points below. |
| 20 min | **Problem set** | Students work in groups of 2 or 3. You walk around and ask questions. |
| 10 min | **Share out** | Students present their solutions at the board, including the ones that went wrong. |

**What you'll need:** paper, pencils, a board, and one printed problem set per student. Calculators stay in the bag, since students can't use them on the AMC 8.

**When students are stuck, try asking:**

- "What is the problem asking for, in your own words?"
- "Can you try it with smaller numbers?"
- "Can you draw it or make a list?"
- "What have you tried so far?"

**Three club rules to share on day one:**

1. Being stuck is normal, and it means you're doing real math.
2. Wrong answers are useful, so we share them.
3. Explaining your thinking counts more than being fast.

## Week 1: Counting

**Goal:** Students learn that competition math is different from class math, and that they can do it.

**Warm-up:** "How many different ways can you line up 3 people for a photo?" Let students act it out.

**Big idea:** When you make one choice and then another, multiply. Use an example from the room, like 2 snack options and 3 drink options, and draw a tree diagram. Our [Counting lesson](/pathway/counting/) walks through this idea, and students can read it on their own.

**Problem set:** [Week 1: Counting](/teachers/problem-sets/week-1/)

## Week 2: Number Sense

**Goal:** Students get comfortable with factors, multiples, primes, and remainders.

**Warm-up:** "Can you share 24 cookies equally among 5 friends? What's left over?"

**Big idea:** Every whole number is built from its factors. Show how to list factor pairs of 24 ($1 \times 24$, $2 \times 12$, $3 \times 8$, $4 \times 6$), stopping when the pairs start to repeat. Our [Number Sense lesson](/pathway/number-sense/) walks through this idea, and students can read it on their own.

**Problem set:** [Week 2: Number Sense](/teachers/problem-sets/week-2/)

## Week 3: Fractions & Ratios

**Goal:** Students see ratios as "groups" instead of a formula to memorize.

**Warm-up:** "A class has 2 boys for every 3 girls, and there are 10 boys. How many girls are there?"

**Big idea:** A ratio of 2 to 3 means groups of 5 people. Draw boxes on the board: 2 boxes for one kind and 3 boxes for the other. Figure out how much goes in each box. Our [Fractions & Ratios lesson](/pathway/fractions-ratios/) walks through this idea, and students can read it on their own.

**Problem set:** [Week 3: Fractions & Ratios](/teachers/problem-sets/week-3/)

## Week 4: Basic Geometry

**Goal:** Students build the habit of always drawing a picture.

**Warm-up:** "Draw a rectangle with perimeter 12. Can you draw a different one?"

**Big idea:** Perimeter is the distance around, and area is the space inside. Have students draw every shape and label what they know before calculating anything. Introduce right triangles and the Pythagorean Theorem, $a^2 + b^2 = c^2$, with the 3-4-5 triangle: $3^2 + 4^2 = 5^2$. Our [Basic Geometry lesson](/pathway/basic-geometry/) walks through these ideas, and students can read it on their own.

**Problem set:** [Week 4: Basic Geometry](/teachers/problem-sets/week-4/)

## Week 5: Logic & Patterns

**Goal:** Students learn to look for patterns and to work backwards.

**Warm-up:** "What's the next number: 2, 4, 8, 16, ...? What about 1, 4, 9, 16, ...?"

**Big idea:** When a problem looks huge, try small cases and look for a pattern. If a problem tells you how something ended, start at the end and undo each step. Our [Logic & Patterns lesson](/pathway/logic-patterns/) walks through these ideas, and students can read it on their own.

**Problem set:** [Week 5: Logic & Patterns](/teachers/problem-sets/week-5/)

## Week 6: Intro to Probability

**Goal:** Students connect probability back to the counting they learned in Week 1.

**Warm-up:** "If you flip a coin, what's the chance of heads? How about flipping two coins and getting two heads? Take a guess, and then we'll test it."

**Big idea:** Probability is the number of ways you want divided by the total number of ways, when each way is equally likely. Have students list all four outcomes for two coins. Our [Intro to Probability lesson](/pathway/intro-probability/) walks through this idea, and students can read it on their own.

**Problem set:** [Week 6: Probability](/teachers/problem-sets/week-6/)

## Week 7: Mini-contest

**Goal:** Students try working under time pressure in a low-stakes way.

This week has a different schedule:

| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 5 min | **Test strategy:** skip hard problems and come back later, answer everything on the AMC 8 since wrong answers cost nothing, and check that your answer makes sense. |
| 20 min | **Mini-contest:** 10 problems, working alone and without calculators. |
| 15 min | **Go over it:** in pairs, compare answers and fix mistakes before you reveal the key. |
| 5 min | **Reflect:** each student writes down one thing that went well and one thing to practice. |

Keep scores private. Students could also retake the contest later to see their improvement.

**Problem set:** [Week 7: Mini-contest](/teachers/problem-sets/week-7/)

## Week 8: Team Round and Celebration

**Goal:** Students finish feeling like a team and know what's next.

**Warm-up:** Let students pick their favorite problem from the past seven weeks and teach it to a partner.

**Big idea:** Hard problems get easier with teammates. Split students into groups of 3 or 4, give each group one copy of the set, and let them divide up the work however they like.

**Share out:** Save the final 10 minutes to celebrate. Recognize effort with fun awards, like "Best Wrong Answer" or "Most Stubborn Problem Solver," and talk about what comes next:

- Sign up for the [AMC 8 or MATHCOUNTS](/teachers/start-a-team/).
- Try some [past AMC 8 problems on the AoPS wiki](https://artofproblemsolving.com/wiki/index.php/AMC_8_Problems_and_Solutions), starting with the early questions.
- Move on to [Level 2 of the Learning Pathway](/pathway/), which goes deeper into the same topics. Level 3 gets students started on AMC 10 ideas.
- Keep the club going, and let returning students help lead it.

**Problem set:** [Week 8: Team Round](/teachers/problem-sets/week-8/)


## Part 2: Weeks 9 to 16

The second half of the plan is for a club that wants to keep going. It moves into Level 2 topics, includes a review week, and runs two mock contests using our free practice tests. The last meeting looks ahead to what comes after the AMC 8.

The meeting shape stays the same: a 5-minute warm-up, 10 minutes on the big idea, 20 minutes on the problem set, and 10 minutes to share. The mock contest weeks use a different schedule, shown below.

## Week 9: Equations & Word Problems

**Goal:** Students turn a story into math they can solve.

**Warm-up:** "I'm thinking of a number. If you double it and add 5, you get 23. What's my number?" Let students guess and check first.

**Big idea:** Name the unknown in words before writing anything, like "let b be the brother's age now." Then write one sentence of the story as an equation. Our [Equations & Word Problems unit](/pathway/equations-word-problems/) walks through this idea.

**Problem set:** [Week 9: Equations & Word Problems](/teachers/problem-sets/week-9/)

## Week 10: Smart Counting

**Goal:** Students learn to spot when something gets counted twice.

**Warm-up:** "How many handshakes happen when 4 people each shake hands once?" Have 4 students act it out.

**Big idea:** When counting groups, ask whether order matters. If it doesn't, count in order and divide out the repeats. When two lists overlap, add them and subtract the overlap. Our [Smart Counting unit](/pathway/smart-counting/) walks through these ideas.

**Problem set:** [Week 10: Smart Counting](/teachers/problem-sets/week-10/)

## Week 11: Primes & Remainders

**Goal:** Students use prime factorizations as a tool.

**Warm-up:** "What's the last digit of $2^{10}$? Write out the powers of 2 until you see a pattern."

**Big idea:** Every whole number breaks into primes in exactly one way, like a fingerprint. Show a factor tree for 72. Our [Primes & Remainders unit](/pathway/primes-remainders/) walks through factors, the GCF and LCM, and remainder patterns.

**Problem set:** [Week 11: Primes & Remainders](/teachers/problem-sets/week-11/)

## Week 12: Review Week

**Goal:** Students practice choosing the right tool when the topic isn't labeled.

**Warm-up:** Write three problems on the board from earlier weeks. Students don't solve them. They only name the topic and the first step.

**Big idea:** On a real contest, nobody tells you the topic. Before solving, ask, "What kind of problem is this, and what tool fits?" Students who want more can try the [Level 1 Mixed Review](/pathway/level-1-review/practice/) or [Level 2 Mixed Review](/pathway/level-2-review/practice/).

**Problem set:** [Week 12: Review Week](/teachers/problem-sets/week-12/)

## Week 13: Mock Contest Day 1

**Goal:** Students take a full-length, timed practice contest in a calm, low-stakes way.

This week uses the [Level 1 Practice Test](/contests/level-1-practice-test/): 25 questions, 40 minutes, in the style of the AMC 8. Students can take it online, or you can print the [worksheet version](/teachers/answer-keys/level-1-practice-test/). Plan for a longer meeting, or split it over two meetings.

| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 5 min | **Warm-up:** the [week 13 warm-up set](/teachers/problem-sets/week-13/), then a quick review of the [contest strategy lesson](/contests/strategy/). |
| 40 min | **Mock contest:** working alone, with no calculators. Remind students that wrong answers cost nothing on the AMC 8, so they should answer every question. |
| 15 min | **Go over it:** students compare answers in pairs and fix mistakes before you reveal the key. |
| 5 min | **Reflect:** each student writes down their score privately, one thing that went well, and one topic to practice. |

Keep scores private. The online version shows each student a "what to review" list, which makes a good homework plan.

**Problem set:** [Week 13: Mock Contest Day 1 Warm-up](/teachers/problem-sets/week-13/)

## Week 14: Triangles & Circles

**Goal:** Students build the habit of labeling every drawing.

**Warm-up:** "A circle has diameter 10. What's its radius? Its circumference?"

**Big idea:** Most geometry mistakes come from mixing up the radius and the diameter, or from not drawing the picture. Have students write "r =" before using any circle formula. Our [Triangles](/pathway/triangles/) and [Circles & Solids](/pathway/circles-solids/) units cover these ideas.

**Problem set:** [Week 14: Triangles & Circles](/teachers/problem-sets/week-14/)

## Week 15: Mock Contest Day 2

**Goal:** Students take a second mock contest and see how much they've grown.

Run this week like week 13, using [Practice Contest A](/contests/practice-contest-a/), which mixes Level 1 and Level 2 ideas. The [worksheet version](/teachers/answer-keys/practice-contest-a/) is on the answer keys page. Start with the [week 15 warm-up set](/teachers/problem-sets/week-15/).

During the reflection, have each student compare this result with week 13. Celebrate any improvement, like answering more questions, finishing faster, or fixing a topic they practiced.

**Problem set:** [Week 15: Mock Contest Day 2 Warm-up](/teachers/problem-sets/week-15/)

## Week 16: What Comes After the AMC 8

**Goal:** Students leave with a plan to keep doing math after the club season ends.

**Warm-up:** Pick one problem from the [week 16 set](/teachers/problem-sets/week-16/) and let students try it before you say anything about it.

**Big idea:** The AMC 8 is a beginning, not an ending. Talk through the paths forward:

- **The AMC 10.** It's the next contest in the same series, for students in grade 10 and below. It has 25 questions in 75 minutes, and the questions go deeper into algebra, counting, and geometry. Read about it on [MAA's AMC page](https://maa.org/student-programs/amc/), and browse [past AMC 10 problems on the AoPS wiki](https://artofproblemsolving.com/wiki/index.php/AMC_10_Problems_and_Solutions). [Level 3 of our Learning Pathway](/pathway/#level-3) is written to get students there.
- **Summer programs.** Many colleges, libraries, and community groups run math camps and math circles in the summer, and some are free or offer financial aid. Ask your school counselor or a nearby college's math department what's available. Always check a program's own site for current costs and dates.
- **Keep practicing.** Students can keep working through the [Learning Pathway](/pathway/) at home, take the [practice contests](/contests/) again, and try the [Problem of the Week](/) on our home page.
- **Keep the club going.** Returning students make great leaders. Ask a few of them to help run next year's first meeting.

**Share out:** End with a celebration, like in week 8. Students who finished every unit in a level can print a free certificate from the [Learning Pathway](/pathway/) page.

**Problem set:** [Week 16: A Taste of the AMC 10](/teachers/problem-sets/week-16/)
