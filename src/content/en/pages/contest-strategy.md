---
title: Contest Strategy
description: How to take a math contest like the AMC 8, from answering every question to pacing your 40 minutes.
---

Knowing the math is only part of a contest. The other part is knowing how to spend 40 minutes. This lesson is about the test itself, and the habits here can raise your score before you learn a single new formula.

> **The big idea:** A contest rewards the questions you get right, not the ones you finish. So spend your time where it earns points, use the answer choices as clues, and never leave a free point on the table.

## 1. Answer every question

On the AMC 8, a correct answer earns 1 point. A wrong answer earns 0, and a blank earns 0 too. That means a guess can never hurt you. Even a wild guess among 5 choices has a 1 in 5 chance of being right.

So before time runs out, **fill in an answer for every question**. If you can rule out even one choice, your guess gets better.

Other contests can have different rules. On the AMC 10, a blank earns a little, so guessing blindly isn't always worth it. Always read the rules for your contest. Our practice contests tell you the scoring before you start.

## 2. Use the answer choices

A multiple-choice contest hands you five possible answers. That's a big clue, and you can use it in two ways.

**Test the choices.** Sometimes it's faster to try each choice in the problem than to solve it from scratch. If a question asks "which number, doubled and added to 9, gives 47?", you can test the choices one at a time.

**Start in the middle.** Choices with numbers are usually listed from smallest to largest. If you test the middle choice and it's too big, the answer must be one of the smaller ones. One test can rule out three choices.

## 3. Estimate to rule out choices

Before you work out an exact answer, ask, "About how big should this be?" For $49 \times 51$, both numbers are near 50, and $50 \times 50 = 2500$. Any choice far from 2,500 is out.

Estimating also catches mistakes. If you're finding the area of a small garden and get 4,000 square feet, something went wrong. Go back and look.

## 4. Skip and come back

The questions usually get harder as you go, but not always. Everyone gets stuck sometimes, even on an early question. If you've spent about two minutes on a question and you don't have a plan, **skip it**. Mark it so you can find it again, and move on.

There's a later question you can solve, and it's worth exactly as much as the one you're stuck on. Coming back with fresh eyes often helps, too. On our practice contests, you can mark a question to come back to, and the numbered list at the top shows which ones you marked.

## 5. Pace your 40 minutes

The AMC 8 has 25 questions in 40 minutes. That's a little over a minute and a half per question, but you don't want to spend it evenly.

- **Questions 1 to 10:** these are often quicker. Aim to finish them in about 10 to 12 minutes, but read every word carefully. Easy questions are where careless mistakes hide.
- **Questions 11 to 20:** give these more time, around 15 to 18 minutes.
- **Questions 21 to 25:** these are the hardest. Try the ones that look possible, and skip the rest.
- **The last 2 minutes:** stop solving and fill in a guess for every question you left blank.

Most first-timers don't finish the AMC 8, and that's normal. A strong first score might come from getting the first 12 or 15 questions right.

## 6. Check your work

When you have time left over, go back over your answers. Some quick checks:

- **Reread the question.** Did it ask for the perimeter or the area? The number of boys or the number of girls? Many wrong answers are right answers to a different question.
- **Plug your answer back in.** If you solved for a number, put it back into the story and see if everything works.
- **Try a small case.** If you used a counting rule, test it on a tiny version of the problem, like the numbers from 1 to 3.
- **Ask if it makes sense.** A probability can't be bigger than 1, and a number of people can't be a fraction.

## Your contest-day checklist

- Answer every question on the AMC 8, even with a guess.
- Look at the answer choices before you start solving.
- Test the middle choice first.
- Estimate first, so you can rule out silly answers.
- Stuck for about two minutes? Mark it and move on.
- Save 2 minutes at the end to fill in every blank.
- Reread each question before you choose an answer.
