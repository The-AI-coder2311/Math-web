**You don't need a competition background to do this.** You don't need to be able to solve every problem, either. What students need most is a regular time, a place to work together, and an adult who says, "Let's figure it out." This kit gives you the rest.

## What's in this kit

- **This guide:** how the two main middle school competitions work and how to sign up.
- **[An 8-week club plan](/teachers/club-plan/):** one 45-minute meeting per week, planned out minute by minute.
- **[Printable problem sets](/teachers/problem-sets/):** a set for every week, with answer keys that explain each solution.

## The two competitions at a glance

| | AMC 8 | MATHCOUNTS Competition Series |
|---|---|---|
| **Run by** | Mathematical Association of America (MAA) | MATHCOUNTS Foundation |
| **Who it's for** | Grade 8 and below, under an age limit set by MAA | Grades 6 to 8 |
| **Format** | One 25-question, 40-minute multiple-choice test | Four rounds: Sprint, Target, Team, and Countdown (Countdown is optional at some levels) |
| **Takes place** | At your school or another approved site, once a year | Starts at your school, then chapter, state, and national levels |
| **Team or solo** | Individual | Individual and team (a school team has 4 students) |
| **Best first step if...** | You want one simple event that any interested student can try | You want a season of practice and a team to travel with |

**Our suggestion:** if you're not sure, start with the club plan, then choose. Both reward the same skills, so nothing your students practice will go to waste.

Rules can change from year to year, and the official sites always have the final word. We don't list dates or fees here because they change. *Format details last checked October 2026.*

## How the AMC 8 works

Students work through 25 questions in 40 minutes. Each correct answer earns one point, and wrong answers cost nothing, so students should answer every question. Calculators aren't allowed. Early questions are approachable for beginners, and later ones get much harder. **Most first-timers won't finish, and that's fine.**

The test covers counting and probability, estimation, proportional reasoning, basic geometry, spatial visualization, and reading graphs and tables.

### How to register your school for the AMC 8

An adult at the school serves as the *competition manager*. Students and parents can't take this role. The steps below are a summary of [MAA's guide for new competition managers](https://maa.org/getting-started-as-a-new-maa-amc-competition-manager/), so follow that guide for the details.

1. **Check eligibility.** Read the [official AMC policies](https://maa.org/student-programs/amc/maa-american-mathematics-competitions-policies/) to confirm that you and your school qualify.
2. **Talk to your administration.** Share why the competition is worthwhile and what it will cost. You can find current [registration deadlines and fees](https://maa.org/amcreg/) on MAA's site. It usually costs less to register early.
3. **Create a competition manager account.** MAA will ask for documents showing that you work at the school, then take some time to review them. **You can't register until this review is done, so start well before the deadline.**
4. **Register and buy student licenses.**
5. **Sign up students, then give the test** on a day you choose during the competition week. You'll proctor it in person.

**If your school can't host:** students can sometimes take the test at another approved site nearby. MAA's [AMC page](https://maa.org/student-programs/amc/) links to a public list of sites.

## How MATHCOUNTS works

MATHCOUNTS has four rounds. The first three are written, and the last is a fast-paced oral round. All of the details below come from the [official Competition Series page](https://www.mathcounts.org/programs/competition-series).

| Round | What students do | Calculator? |
|---|---|---|
| **Sprint** | 30 problems in 40 minutes, working alone | No |
| **Target** | 4 pairs of problems, with 6 minutes per pair, working alone | Yes |
| **Team** | 10 problems in 20 minutes, working as a team of 4 | Yes |
| **Countdown** | Quick head-to-head questions, up to 45 seconds each | No |

Your school holds its own competition first, then you pick who moves on. Top students can advance from the chapter level to state and national competitions.

### How to register your school for MATHCOUNTS

1. Read the [Competition Series page](https://www.mathcounts.org/programs/competition-series) for this year's rules, dates, and fees.
2. [Register your school](https://www.mathcounts.org/reg) through the MATHCOUNTS site.
3. Use the materials MATHCOUNTS gives you to prepare students and run your school competition.
4. Send your team and any individual competitors to your chapter competition.

### Not ready for competition? Try a club first

The [MATHCOUNTS National Math Club](https://www.mathcounts.org/programs/national-math-club) is a non-competitive program for grades 6 to 8, with free games, explorations, and problem sets. It works well alongside our club plan.

## Common worries

**"I can't solve the hard problems."** You don't have to. Say, "I'm not sure yet. What have you tried?" Students learn a lot from watching an adult stay curious when stuck. Every answer key on this site explains the reasoning, so you can read it before the meeting.

**"My students will be discouraged by low scores."** Tell them ahead of time that these contests are much harder than class tests, and that even strong students miss many questions. Measure success by improvement and effort, not by rank.

**"Only a few students are interested."** That's enough. Three students and a whiteboard make a math team. Ask students to bring a friend, and invite students who like puzzles, not just those with top grades.

**"We can't afford the fees."** Start with the free club plan, then talk with your administration or parent group about costs. You can also ask the competition organizers directly whether any support is available.
