Everyone should be able to learn here. We're aiming for the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.1 at level AA. We check every page with automated tools each time we make accessibility changes, but tools can't catch everything, so we still want to hear from you.

## What we support

- **Screen readers.** Pages use real headings, lists, tables, and landmarks, and every page has a "Skip to main content" link at the top.
- **Math.** Formulas include MathML, which many screen readers can read aloud. How well this works depends on your screen reader and its settings.
- **Pictures.** Every diagram has a written description of what it shows.
- **Keyboards.** You can reach every control with the Tab key, and the current one has a visible outline. Enter or Space opens a hint or a solution, and Escape closes it.
- **Right and wrong answers.** Feedback always uses words and a symbol, never color alone. Messages are announced to screen readers when they appear.
- **Zoom.** Pages work when zoomed to 200%. A wide table or a long formula may scroll sideways inside its own box.
- **Color and motion.** Text meets contrast guidelines in both the light and dark themes, and the site follows your device's setting for reduced motion.
- **Phones and tablets.** Buttons and other controls are large enough to tap.
- **Timers.** The timer on a practice contest is optional. You can turn it off at any time, and it never ends your contest.
- **Printing.** Lessons, worksheets, and problem sets print on a white background, without menus or buttons.

## What we know isn't perfect

- Diagrams are pictures, so their descriptions take the place of exploring them by touch or sound.
- Some long formulas need sideways scrolling on a small screen.
- Each formula's spoken wording comes from your screen reader, and some wording may sound unusual.
