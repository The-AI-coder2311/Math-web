---
title: Tips for starting a math club at your school
description: Practical advice for starting a school math club.
---

- **Pick one regular time.** A short meeting every week, in the same room, matters more than a long one now and then. Before school, lunch, and after school can each work, so ask students what fits their buses and jobs.
- **Find a sponsor and tell the office.** You may need a staff sponsor and a room. A short note to your principal also helps later, when you ask about contest registration.
- **Invite students directly.** Many students won't sign up for a "math team," but they'll come if a teacher says, "I think you'd like this." Invite students who like puzzles, not only those with top grades.
- **Make it easy to attend.** Keep the club free, with no tryouts. Let students come when they can, and give anyone who misses a week an easy way to catch up.
- **Plan for mixed levels.** Group students so that everyone talks, and keep a harder problem ready for those who finish early. Returning students can help lead.
- **Start with a win.** Choose first-week problems that everyone can do something with. Students come back when they leave feeling capable.
- **Celebrate effort, not rank.** Keep scores private, and praise good questions, new strategies, and sticking with a hard problem.
- **Check in with families.** Tell parents and guardians what the club is, that it's free, and when it meets. Parents can use these lessons at home too.
- **Plan for devices.** Lessons work on phones and Chromebooks. If students don't have devices, print the worksheets and use a single projector.
