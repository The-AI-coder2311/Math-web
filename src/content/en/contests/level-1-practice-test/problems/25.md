A bag holds 3 red marbles and 2 blue marbles. You take out two marbles, one after the other, without putting the first one back. What is the probability that both marbles are the same color?

## Solution

There are two ways to match: both red or both blue. Use a tree and multiply along each branch.

- **Both red:** the first is red with probability $\frac{3}{5}$. Then 2 of the 4 marbles left are red, so the second is red with probability $\frac{2}{4}$. Multiply: $\frac{3}{5} \times \frac{2}{4} = \frac{6}{20}$.
- **Both blue:** $\frac{2}{5} \times \frac{1}{4} = \frac{2}{20}$.

Add the two branches: $\frac{6}{20} + \frac{2}{20} = \frac{8}{20} = \frac{2}{5}$.

If you got $\frac{13}{25}$, you put the first marble back. Here it stays out, so the second draw has only 4 marbles.

**Answer: (A) $\frac{2}{5}$**
