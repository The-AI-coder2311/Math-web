A frog climbs a staircase with 10 steps. Each hop goes up either 2 steps or 3 steps, and the frog must land exactly on the top step. In how many different orders of hops can the frog reach the top? (For example, 2, 2, 3, 3 is different from 3, 3, 2, 2.)

## Solution

Split into cases by how many 3-step hops the frog takes. The hops must add up to 10.

- **No 3-step hops:** five 2-step hops. There's only 1 order.
- **One 3-step hop:** the other 7 steps would need 2-step hops, but 7 is odd. This can't happen.
- **Two 3-step hops:** that's 6 steps, so two 2-step hops make the other 4. The four hops are 3, 3, 2, 2 in some order. Choose which 2 of the 4 hops are the 3s: $\frac{4 \times 3}{2} = 6$ orders.
- **Three 3-step hops:** that's 9 steps, and 1 step can't be made from 2s. This can't happen.

In total: $1 + 6 = 7$.

**Answer: (C) 7**
