A \$40 game goes on sale for 50% off. A week later, the store raises the sale price by 50%. Is the game back to \$40? It feels like it should be, since it went down by 50% and up by 50%. Work it out, and you'll find that it's only \$30. Where did the \$10 go? This lesson will explain it.

> **The big idea:** A percent means "out of 100," and it's always a percent **of something**. Before you calculate, ask, "A percent of what?"

## Why percents work

The word *percent* means "per hundred." So 15% is the fraction $\frac{15}{100}$. Percents are useful because they put everything on the same scale. Is 18 out of 25 better than 33 out of 50? Written as percents, those are 72% and 66%, so it's easy to compare.

You can find most percents in your head by building them from easy pieces:

- 50% is half.
- 25% is a quarter.
- 10% means dividing by 10.
- 1% means dividing by 100.

For example, to find 35% of 80, start with 10% of 80, which is 8. Then 30% is $3 \times 8 = 24$, and 5% is half of 8, or 4. So 35% of 80 is $24 + 4 = 28$.

## Example 1: Finding a percent of a number

A team played 60 games and won 45% of them. How many did it win?

**What's the plan?** Build 45% from easy pieces. Since 10% of 60 is 6, we know that 40% is 24. Also, 5% is half of 6, which is 3. So 45% is $24 + 3 = 27$ games.

Here's a neat trick that can help too: $a$% of $b$ is the same as $b$% of $a$. For example, 8% of 50 looks hard, but 50% of 8 is 4. Those are equal, since both are $8 \times 50 \div 100$.

Try this one. What's 16% of 25?

<details class="check"><summary>Show the answer</summary>

Swap them: 25% of 16 is a quarter of 16, which is 4.

</details>

## Example 2: What percent is it?

A class has 40 students, and 14 of them walk to school. What percent walk?

**What's the question really asking?** We want to rewrite $\frac{14}{40}$ as something out of 100. To turn 40 into 100, multiply by 2.5. Do the same to the top: $14 \times 2.5 = 35$. So it's $\frac{35}{100}$, or 35%.

## Example 3: Percent increase and decrease

A shirt costs \$60, and the price goes up by 15%. What's the new price?

**What do we know?** The increase is 15% of 60. Since 10% is 6 and 5% is 3, the increase is 9. So the new price is $60 + 9 = 69$ dollars.

There's a one-step way too. If the price goes up 15%, then the new price is 115% of the old price: $1.15 \times 60 = 69$. Going down by 20% means that 80% is left, so you can multiply by 0.8.

Now the opening puzzle makes sense. The price of \$40 goes down 50%, to \$20. Then it goes up 50%, but that's 50% of **20**, which is only 10. So it ends at \$30. The two percents were of different numbers.

## Example 4: Working backwards

After a 20% discount, a backpack costs \$48. What was the original price?

**What do we know?** The \$48 is what's left, and that's 80% of the original.

**What's the plan?** Think of it in boxes. If 80% is 48, then 10% is $48 \div 8 = 6$. So 100% is $10 \times 6 = 60$. The original price was \$60.

**Check:** 20% of 60 is 12, and $60 - 12 = 48$.

## Example 5: A contest-style question

A rectangle's length is increased by 20%, and its width is decreased by 10%. By what percent does its area change?

**What do we know?** We don't know the sides of the rectangle, but the answer doesn't depend on them.

**Can we pick friendly numbers?** Yes. Let the rectangle be 10 by 10, so its area is 100. That's a nice number for percents.

- The new length is 12, since it went up 20%.
- The new width is 9, since it went down 10%.
- The new area is $12 \times 9 = 108$.

The area went from 100 to 108, which is an increase of **8%**. Notice that the answer isn't $20 - 10 = 10$. Percents don't add up that way when they multiply each other.

## Going deeper

In mixture problems, one amount usually stays fixed while the percent changes. Find it first.

**A harder one:** A 50-gram mixture is 20% salt. How much water do you need to add to make it 10% salt?

**What stays the same?** Adding water doesn't change the salt. There are $0.2 \times 50 = 10$ grams of salt, before and after.

**What must the new total be?** The 10 grams must be 10% of the new mixture. Since 10% of 100 is 10, the new total is 100 grams.

**How much did we add?** $100 - 50 = 50$ grams of water.

Notice that cutting the percent in half took **doubling** the total. That makes sense, since the same salt is spread through twice as much mixture.

**The trap:** Many people subtract the percents, or take 10% of 50. Neither one tracks the salt. Write down what stays fixed before you do any other math.

Try this. A 40-liter drink is 25% juice. How much water do you add to make it 20% juice?

<details class="check"><summary>Show the answer</summary>

There are $0.25 \times 40 = 10$ liters of juice. If 10 liters is 20% of the new total, then the new total is 50 liters. So you add 10 liters of water.

</details>

## Common traps

**Thinking that up 50% and down 50% cancel.** They don't, since the second percent is of a different number. The same goes for two increases: up 10% and then up 10% again is a total increase of 21%, not 20%.

**Taking a percent of the wrong number.** In Example 4, some students add 20% of 48 to 48 and get \$57.60. But the 20% was taken from the **original** price, not the sale price.

**Answering with the wrong thing.** If a problem asks for the discount, don't answer with the sale price. In Example 3, the increase is 9, and the new price is 69. Test writers often include both as choices.

</section>

<section data-box="strategy">

## Contest strategy

**How to spot it:** Look for %, "discount," "increase," "tax," "tip," or "percent of."

**Use 100 as your starting number:** When no amount is given, start with 100. Then every percent is easy.

**Use the choices:** For working-backwards problems, test the answer choices. Take 20% off each one, and see which gives 48.

**Time:** One to two minutes. Multiplying by a decimal, like 0.8, is often the quickest for repeated changes.

## Remember this

- Percent means "out of 100."
- Build from 50%, 25%, 10%, and 1%.
- Always ask, "A percent of what?"
- Up 15% means multiply by 1.15. Down 20% means multiply by 0.8.
- No numbers given? Start with 100.
