Leo spends half of his money on lunch. Then he spends \$3 on a snack. He has \$5 left. How much did he start with? You could guess and check. You could write an equation. Or you could start at the end, where you know exactly how much he has, and walk back through time.

> **The big idea:** When a problem tells you how something **ended**, start at the end. Undo each step, in **reverse** order, using the opposite operation.

## Why it works

Think about getting dressed. You put on socks, and then shoes. To undo that, you take off the shoes first, and then the socks. Undoing happens in the opposite order, and each step gets replaced by its opposite.

| Forward step | How to undo it |
|---|---|
| Add 3 | Subtract 3 |
| Subtract 3 | Add 3 |
| Multiply by 2 | Divide by 2 |
| Take half | Double |

Let's try it on Leo's money:

- He ended with \$5.
- Undo the snack by adding back 3: $5 + 3 = 8$ dollars.
- He had \$8 after taking half for lunch. So undo that by doubling: $8 \times 2 = 16$ dollars.

**Check it forward:** Half of \$16 is \$8, and $8 - 3 = 5$. It works. Checking takes only a few seconds, since you're running the story the normal way.

## Example 1: A number puzzle

Ty thinks of a number, multiplies it by 3, and subtracts 4. The result is 20. What was the number?

**What do we know?** The ending value is 20, and there are two steps.

**Undo them in reverse order.**

- Undo "subtract 4" by adding 4: $20 + 4 = 24$.
- Undo "multiply by 3" by dividing by 3: $24 \div 3 = 8$.

**Check:** $8 \times 3 - 4 = 20$.

Try this one on your own. Nia adds 7 to a number, and then doubles the result. She gets 30. What number did she start with?

<details class="check"><summary>Show the answer</summary>

Undo the doubling first: $30 \div 2 = 15$. Then undo adding 7: $15 - 7 = 8$. Check: $8 + 7 = 15$, and $15 \times 2 = 30$.

</details>

## Example 2: A fraction of what's left

A bakery sells $\frac{1}{3}$ of its muffins in the morning, and then $\frac{1}{2}$ of the remaining muffins in the afternoon. It has 10 muffins left. How many did it start with?

**What do we know?** Fractions make forward thinking messy, since we don't know the start. Working backwards keeps everything in whole numbers.

- At the end, there are 10. Selling $\frac{1}{2}$ left the other half, so before the afternoon, there were $10 \times 2 = 20$.
- Those 20 were what remained after selling $\frac{1}{3}$. That means 20 is $\frac{2}{3}$ of the start. If 2 groups are 20, then one group is 10, and all 3 groups make 30.

So the bakery started with 30 muffins.

**Check:** It sells 10 of the 30 in the morning, which leaves 20. It sells half of those, which leaves 10.

> **Tip:** "Sold $\frac{1}{3}$" means that $\frac{2}{3}$ remain. To undo that, divide by the fraction that's left, not the fraction that's gone.

## Example 3: Exchanges between people

Ann and Bo have some cards. First, Ann gives Bo 5 cards. Then, Bo gives Ann 2 cards. Now they each have 20 cards. How many did Ann start with?

**What's the plan?** Keep track of both people in a table, and walk it backwards from the bottom.

| Moment | Ann | Bo |
|---|---|---|
| The end | 20 | 20 |
| Before Bo gave 2 to Ann | 18 | 22 |
| Before Ann gave 5 to Bo | 23 | 17 |

So Ann started with 23 cards. Notice that the total stays 40 on every line. That's a useful check whenever things only move between people.

## Example 4: A contest-style question

A frog is at the bottom of a staircase. Each time it hops, it does this: it **doubles** the number of the step it's on, and then adds 1. So from step 0, it hops to step 1, and then to step 3. After some hops, it lands on step 63. If it started on step 0, how many hops did it take?

**What's the plan?** Work backwards. To undo "double and add 1," subtract 1 and then divide by 2.

- From 63: $62 \div 2 = 31$.
- From 31: $30 \div 2 = 15$.
- From 15: $14 \div 2 = 7$.
- From 7: $6 \div 2 = 3$.
- From 3: $2 \div 2 = 1$.
- From 1: $0 \div 2 = 0$.

That's 6 steps back to 0, so it took **6 hops**. It would've been hard to go forward without knowing when to stop, but going backwards leaves no choices.

<section data-box="traps">

## Common traps

**Undoing in the wrong order.** In Example 1, some students divide by 3 first: $20 \div 3$ doesn't even come out to a whole number. The last thing that happened gets undone **first**.

**Using the same operation instead of the opposite.** To undo "add 4," you subtract 4. It's easy to slip when you're moving quickly, and a forward check catches it.

**Treating "half of what's left" as half of the original.** In Example 2, the $\frac{1}{2}$ was taken from what remained after the morning. Working backwards one step at a time handles this for you.

</section>

## Contest strategy

**How to spot it:** The final value is given, and you're asked about the start. Words like "started with," "originally," and "at first" are good clues.

**Always check forward.** It takes 15 seconds, and it catches almost every mistake.

**Test the choices:** Since the question has a starting value, you can run an answer choice forward through the story. Try the middle choice first. If it comes out too big or too small, that tells you which way to go.

**Time:** About a minute.

## Remember this

- Know the ending? Start there.
- Undo steps in reverse order, using opposites.
- "Sold $\frac{1}{3}$" means $\frac{2}{3}$ remain.
- A table helps when there are several people.
- Check forward through the story.
